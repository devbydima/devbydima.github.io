Whoa, What Changed?

Last updated: 15 November 2026

Whoa, What Changed? is a memory game for Android in which you look at a picture and spot what changed. This page explains what information the app uses and why. The short version: the game keeps your progress on your own phone, we have no accounts, and the information collected through the app goes to Google (for ads and anonymous usage statistics) and to RevenueCat (to handle purchases).

Who we are

The app is made by devbydima. You can reach us at devbydima@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

Your progress (stars, scores, stickers, hints, streaks, settings, the language you picked) is stored on your phone. We never receive it. If you use Android’s backup feature, your phone may include this data in your Google backup; that backup belongs to your Google account and is handled by Google, not by us. If you uninstall the app or choose Reset progress in the settings, it is removed from your phone.

What we do not do

  • There are no accounts and no sign-in.
  • We do not run tracking of our own, and we do not collect your name, e-mail address, location, contacts, photos or microphone. The anonymous usage statistics are described under Usage statistics below.
  • We do not sell your information.

Sharing

The share buttons open your phone’s share menu. Sharing only happens when you tap a button and pick an app yourself. A picture you share is kept briefly in the app’s temporary cache on your phone. Nothing is sent to us.

Ads

The app shows ads through Google AdMob: videos you choose to watch in return for a reward (such as a hint or an extra try), and now and then a full-screen ad between levels. To show and measure ads, Google may collect and use information from your device, such as your advertising ID, IP address, approximate location, device and app information, and how you interact with ads. We do not receive this information. How Google handles it is described at policies.google.com/technologies/ads and in Google’s Privacy Policy.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or another region that requires it, the app asks for your consent before showing personalised ads, using Google’s consent form. You can change your choice at any time under Settings → Privacy options. On Android you can also reset your advertising ID or opt out of ad personalisation in your phone’s settings (Settings → Google → Ads).

Usage statistics

The app uses Google Firebase Analytics to learn in general how the game is used, for example which levels are started and finished, how often ads are shown and how often purchases are tried. Firebase may collect an app instance ID, device and app information (such as model, Android version and language), approximate location derived from your IP address, and these events. It is not linked to your name or e-mail address, because the app has no accounts. We use it only to improve the game. How Google handles it is described at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or another region that requires it, usage statistics stay switched off until you agree in Google’s consent form. You can change your choice at any time under Settings → Privacy options.

Purchases

You can buy “Remove ads” once, inside the app. You can also buy packs of hints and streak freezes. Payment is handled by Google Play: we never see your payment details, name or email address. The app uses RevenueCat (a service that manages in-app purchases for us) to check what has been bought, so that your hints and streak freezes arrive and the full-screen ads can be switched off again on a new phone or after a reinstall. RevenueCat receives an anonymous ID created for the app, your purchase records and technical information such as your IP address and device type; it is not linked to your name or e-mail address. See RevenueCat’s Privacy Policy and Google’s Privacy Policy.

Legal basis and Google’s role (EEA, UK)

We do not process personal information ourselves. For the advertising and usage-statistics information described above, Google acts as an independent controller, and the legal basis is your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you give or refuse in Google’s consent form. You can withdraw it at any time under Settings → Privacy options. Google may process this information in countries outside the EEA and the UK, under the safeguards described in Google’s own policies.

Notifications

If you turn on the daily reminder, the app schedules a notification on your phone at the time you chose. It is created by the app itself on your phone: nothing is sent from a server, and no information leaves your device for this. You can turn it off in the settings or in your phone’s notification settings at any time.

Age

Whoa, What Changed? is intended for people aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has used the app, contact us and we will help.

How long information is kept

We keep nothing: all game data is on your phone and is removed when you uninstall the app. Information that Google collects for ads and usage statistics, and information that RevenueCat holds about purchases, is kept by them under their own policies.

Your rights

Because we hold no personal information about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete. For information that Google holds, you can use the tools at myaccount.google.com. For information that RevenueCat holds, see its privacy policy. If you have a question about privacy, write to us at the address above.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, you also have the right to withdraw your consent (see above) and to lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority, for example the Dutch Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl).

US state privacy laws

We do not sell personal information and we do not collect personal information ourselves. Advertising information processed by Google through the app may count as “sharing” or “targeted advertising” under some US state laws. You can opt out of personalised ads in the app under Settings → Privacy options (where shown) or in your phone’s ad settings (Settings → Google → Ads).

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update this page and the date at the top. Material changes will also be mentioned in the app’s update notes.
